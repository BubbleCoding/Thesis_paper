{\Large
\noindent
{\bf Abstract}} \\
\vspace*{0.3 cm}

\noindent
Storytelling, and by extension retelling, has continuously evolved alongside humanity, adapting to and incorporating new technologies. This thesis explores how locally inferred artificial intelligence models can be used to advance this evolution by retelling tabletop role-playing games in the form of comic books. As a case study, a multimodal AI pipeline was developed to transform audio recordings of Dungeons \& Dragons sessions into fully generated comic books. The pipeline integrates transcription, script generation, image generation, and layout assembly, and was built following a design science research approach. Its effectiveness and impact was evaluated through a user case study combining qualitative and quantitative questionnaire data, complemented by self-observation and data analysis. The findings reveal key design requirements for AI-based retelling systems and demonstrate that generative AI holds significant potential for transforming how games are retold. At the same time, the research highlights substantial challenges that must be addressed such as story coherence, ethics, and user agency for storytelling technologies to continue evolving in this direction.
