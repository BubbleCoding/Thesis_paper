The chapter begins by introducing the overarching framework of \textit{Computing as a Discipline} and how it informs the design and evaluation of the artefact. \section{Computing as a Discipline}
This thesis adopts the framework of \textit{Computing as a Discipline} \cite{denning1989computing} as its primary research approach. This framework enables a systematic and scientific investigation centred on the development of digital artefacts. Within this framework, the design paradigm is used, which consists of four iterative steps:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{State requirements} - Identify the goals, constraints, and desired outcomes of the system.
\item \textbf{State specifications} - Translate the requirements into measurable and testable criteria.
\item \textbf{Design and implement the system} - Develop the architecture and build the working prototype or tool.
\item \textbf{Test the system} - Evaluate the system against the specifications through testing or case studies.
\end{enumerate}

In Step 1, \textit{State requirements}, this thesis addresses the need to define design requirements for a system that uses artificial intelligence to retell gaming sessions. TTRPGs are chosen as the basis due to their rich narrative structure and the absence of a complex digital system. This choice reduces the system's scope and complexity, focusing on story-based retellings instead of interactive gameplay or video capture.

In Step 2, \textit{State specifications}, the system’s requirements are translated into a set of functional and non-functional specifications that guide the design and implementation.

The functional specifications are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item The system must transcribe audio from a single-microphone recording of a TTRPG session.
\item It must generate a narrative script based on the transcribed text.
\item It must generate visual comic panels that align with the script's events and dialogue.
\item It must compile the script and images into a comic book format suitable for reading.
\end{itemize}

The non-functional specifications include:
\begin{itemize}
\item The output must be understandable without prior knowledge of the session.
\item The system must run fully automatically, with no manual artistic or editorial intervention.
\item The comic must reflect key narrative moments, character presence, and setting changes.
\end{itemize}

The system is scoped to retell sessions from tabletop role-playing games and does not attempt to represent mechanics, dice rolls, or real-time interactions. Its focus lies in retelling emergent stories visually, using artificial intelligence as a generative tool.

In Step 3, \textit{Design and implement the system}, the artefact is developed using an iterative design process aligned with the Design Science Research (DSR) methodology. Before conducting any evaluation, the full AI-driven pipeline must be constructed. This pipeline includes modules for transcription, script generation, and image generation. The specifics of this development process are detailed in the next section.

In Step 4, \textit{Test the system}, the complete pipeline is evaluated in a case study by converting multiple TTRPG session recordings into comic books. No manual corrections or tweaks are made to the output, as the goal is not to fine-tune a specific model but to assess the pipeline holistically. The aim is to uncover design requirements that inform future development of AI-based game retelling tools.

To ensure consistency during testing, a single Game Master runs the same TTRPG session multiple times with different groups of players. This creates a controlled environment while still allowing for natural variation. The results are cross-referenced to observe how the system handles minor differences between groups, pacing, and content across recordings.

Participants provide both qualitative and quantitative feedback via semi-structured interviews after reviewing the generated comic. Additional expert feedback is collected from multiple GMs who evaluate the system's output and usability. This multi-perspective evaluation helps identify strengths, weaknesses, and areas for improvement in the artefact.

\section{Design Science Research as a Methodology}

It is a systematic approach that emphasises the creation and evaluation of innovative artefacts to solve real-world problems, primarily used within the engineering disciplines. As this thesis aims to create a novel artefact and as I am deeply familiar with the DSR methodology, it will be the primary methodology used in this thesis. There are different versions/interpretations of DSR, but the one used in this thesis is structured around 7 different guidelines \cite{hevner2004design} which can be seen in Table \ref{tab:DSRGuidelines}.

\subsection*{Guideline 1: Design as an Artifact}
The central contribution of this thesis is a working artefact: a multimodal AI pipeline that transforms TTRPG session audio recordings into comic books. This includes transcription, transcript-to-script conversion, image generation, and layout formatting components.

\subsection*{Guideline 2: Problem Relevance}
The problem addressed is the lack of AI-based tools for automatically retelling gaming experiences, particularly those rooted in narrative-heavy analogue formats like TTRPGs. This project explores how artificial intelligence can support the automated retelling and archiving of such experiences. The resulting insights aim to inform the design of future game retelling systems and contribute to the broader field of AI-assisted narrative generation.

\subsection*{Guideline 3: Design Evaluation}
The system is evaluated through case studies in which the same TTRPG session is run multiple times by the same Game Master but played by different groups. This setup ensures narrative consistency, enabling direct cross-referencing of the resulting comics. The outputs are reviewed by session participants and expert Game Masters, combining qualitative user feedback with functional evaluation to assess the system's effectiveness.

\subsection*{Guideline 4: Research Contributions}
This thesis contributes a novel tool for AI-assisted narrative retelling, along with insights into the design requirements for such systems. It offers a practical demonstration of integrating generative models into a creative pipeline and contributes to ongoing discussions about how games can be represented and retold after play.

\subsection*{Guideline 5: Research Rigor}
A rigorous approach is applied to both the design and evaluation of the artefact. The development process follows established software engineering practices, including iterative prototyping, testing, and refinement. The selection and use of AI models are grounded in documented performance and methodological consistency.

\subsection*{Guideline 6: Design as a Search Process}
The artefact could be constructed in countless ways by varying models, prompts, and parameters. Rather than attempting exhaustive optimisation, this thesis seeks to develop a satisfactory and functional solution from which useful design requirements can be derived. This allows the project to contribute broadly applicable insights without being constrained to a single ideal configuration.

\subsection*{Guideline 7: Communication of Research}
This thesis is written to be accessible to both technical and non-technical audiences. It includes detailed descriptions of the design process, system architecture, and evaluation methodology. In addition, visual examples of the generated comics are included to concretely demonstrate the system's capabilities and outcomes.

By following these 7 guidelines, this thesis aims to produce a meaningful contribution to the field of AI-assisted narrative generation, while also adhering to the principles of design science research.

\section{Data Collection Methods} \label{sec:DataCollection}
This section discusses the methods used to evaluate the usability and effectiveness of the developed system. A combination of qualitative and quantitative techniques will be employed, including a case study, self-observation, and expert review. These methods aim to uncover strengths and limitations in the system, with a focus on identifying concrete design requirements for a narrative retelling tool in the context of TTRPGs.

\subsection*{Case Study}
The first step in the evaluation process involves generating a number of comic books using the system. To do this, a case study will be conducted in which a single Game Master runs the same TTRPG session multiple times with different groups of players. The session will be run with 2 or 3 players and will last between 1 and 2 hours. This session length is chosen to avoid the need for breaks during the session, which should result in less requirement for post-processing and a more streamlined recording. The game used will be \textit{Dungeons and Dragons 5th Edition} as this is a system which is well known by players and it is easy to find participants already familiar with its rules. Furthermore, it is important that the players are knowledgeable about the game, as this minimises rule-related interruptions, simplifying the session recordings.

The session will be recorded in a manner which is true to how players would normally go about recording a session, using a smartphone. The smartphone used is a Samsung Flip 5, as this is the phone owned by the Game Master. Using the same phone creates consistent recordings, which is important for consistent results. The primary reason as to why a smartphone is used is because it would be uncommon for a random TTRPG group to have a high-quality microphone available. The location of the recording will be in the same private home of the Game Master, as this is a natural location for a TTRPG session to take place. The entire setup has been chosen to keep the ecological validity of TTRPG sessions as high as possible \cite{kurniawan2004interaction}. By staying true to real-world conditions, the evaluation remains grounded and realistic.

After the sessions have been conducted, the recordings will be processed by the system to generate a comic book. The comic book will then be presented to the players of the session. Participants will be asked to complete a questionnaire containing both closed (quantitative) and open (qualitative) questions, allowing for a well-rounded understanding of how the comic book was received.

\subsection*{Self-Observation}
The comic books generated by the system will also be evaluated by the researcher. The purpose of this is to cross-reference the comic books with each other to identify major deviations in output across identical input sessions. As we are using artificial intelligence to generate the comic books, a high degree of variation is expected due to the random nature of AI generation. This will also make it possible to see if the system has issues with certain aspects of a session and if there are consistent problems at certain points of the session. Primary aspects looked at will be the overall narrative, text, characters, art style, completeness, and the overall quality of the comic book. The researcher will also look at the comic books from a technical perspective, assessing system performance in transcription, script generation, and image generation. This process helps identify both strengths and areas for improvement within the pipeline.

The key focus of the self-observation is understanding what makes the output feel, or fail to feel, like a comic book. As the purpose of this thesis is to identify design requirements for a retelling tool, it is important to discover these requirements by cross-referencing the comic books with each other, with other comic books, and with the input of the participants of the case study.

\subsection*{Expert Review}
The last form of evaluation for this thesis will be an expert review in the form of a semi-structured interview with multiple Game Masters. The purpose of this is to link the narrative structure and components of a TTRPG session to the comic book. This way, we can see if the important aspects of a TTRPG session are represented in the comic book and if the comic book is a good representation of the session. Furthermore, this will also create a greater understanding of what should be part of a retelling of a game session, as the Game Masters will be able to give feedback on what is missing and what is present in the current work, allowing for a more complete understanding of the design requirements of a retelling tool.

In addition, a semi-structured interview will be conducted with the Game Master who ran the recorded sessions. This provides valuable insights into the intended narrative and supports more accurate self-observation. It also reduces the need for the researcher to manually parse and cross-reference all session details. This interview also provides a strong reference point to support the self-observation process.

\section{Data Analysis Methods}
This section outlines how the collected data will be analysed to evaluate the system and extract concrete design requirements for a TTRPG retelling tool. Both qualitative and quantitative analysis techniques will be used to interpret the results from the case study, self-observation, and expert review.

\subsection*{Case Study Analysis}
The case study will provide both quantitative and qualitative data through questionnaires completed by players after reviewing the generated comic book. The quantitative data will primarily come from closed-ended questions, which include Likert-scale items evaluating aspects such as clarity, enjoyment, narrative coherence, and visual quality. The responses will be summarised using descriptive statistics, including averages, standard deviation, and frequency distribution. This data will help identify the overall player perspective on the artefact and clarify which aspects of the comic book were particularly successful or in need of improvement.

The qualitative data will come from the open-ended questions in the player questionnaires. The answers given by the participants will be analysed using thematic analysis. It is expected that the identified themes will inform concrete design requirements for a narrative retelling tool. Thematic analysis will involve coding the responses to identify recurring themes, patterns, and insights related to the comic book's design requirements. This qualitative analysis will provide a deeper understanding of player experiences and expectations, complementing the quantitative findings.

\subsection*{Self-Observation Analysis}
A significant portion of the analysis will be carried out by the researcher through self-observation of the comic books generated by the system and the transcripts of the play sessions. This will be done in a structured manner, beginning with a general review of the comics and transcripts while making minor notes. Following this, key aspects such as narrative structure, consistency across sessions, visual quality, character representation, and overall completeness will be assessed. These observations will be documented and compared across the different generated comics to identify patterns in system performance and points of failure.

The self-observation will also include a technical analysis of the system, in which the researcher examines the individual outputs of each stage in the pipeline to identify major issues. This helps determine whether any shortcomings in the final comic book stem from the input recording, the system's interpretation, or the image generation stage.

\subsection*{Expert Review Analysis}
The interviews with the Game Masters will follow a similar approach to the player questionnaires. The interview will be recorded, transcribed, and coded for a thematic analysis by hand. Common themes, patterns, and insights will be identified to understand how well the comic book represents the TTRPG session and what design requirements emerge from expert perspectives. The analysis will focus on finding design requirements for a retelling tool as per the research question of this thesis. These interviews are expected to provide valuable insights, as the researcher can engage in an open-ended conversation with experts on the topic.

Additionally, the transcripts will be analysed using an AI-based tool as an exploratory step. While not central to the evaluation, this secondary analysis may reinforce the manual thematic findings and offer insights into the potential of AI-assisted qualitative analysis.

\section{Ethical Considerations}
As this thesis involves human participants who are recorded, it is important to consider the ethical implications of the research. Participants will be informed about the purpose of the study, what participation entails, and how their data will be used. They will be made aware that participation is voluntary and that they may withdraw from the study at any point without any consequences.

The anonymity of the participants will be preserved by not using any personal information in the thesis. Recordings, questionnaires, and all other data will be anonymised and processed exclusively using offline tools. No data will be stored on cloud platforms. Participants will also be informed that they may request the deletion of their data at any time. All collected data will be permanently deleted after the thesis is completed and will not be used for any purpose beyond this research.

Given that this project makes extensive use of artificial intelligence, there is a possibility that participants may be misrepresented by the system. Participants will be explicitly informed that the output is fully AI-generated and that the system is imperfect. They will be warned that the comic book adaptation may diverge from their expectations and that there is a potential for unintentional misrepresentation or emotional discomfort.

Every effort will be made to minimise these risks through transparency, clear communication, and participant agency in the process.

\section{Limitations}
All research methods have their limitations, and this thesis is no different. This section outlines the limitations of the chosen methods and how they may affect the results and generalisability of the findings.

\subsection*{Case Study Limitations}
The case study approach used in this thesis has several limitations that should be acknowledged. Firstly, the results may not be generalisable to all TTRPG systems or play experiences. The study focuses on a single TTRPG session, using one system and one Game Master. This significantly reduces variation, which limits the applicability of the findings beyond this specific setup. Secondly, the use of a single Game Master may introduce bias due to their individual play style and narrative structure. Finally, since the sessions were limited in duration and designed as short one-shots, they do not capture the complexity of long-form campaigns, which often involve evolving character arcs and emergent storytelling over extended periods.

\subsection*{Self-Observation Limitations}
The self-observation method provides valuable insight into the design requirements for a retelling system, but it is inherently subjective. As the researcher is also the system designer, there is a risk of bias in how issues are identified, interpreted, or prioritised. Although structured note-taking and consistent review criteria are used, the lack of a second reviewer or formal coding system may affect reliability. Furthermore, while the researcher is knowledgeable about games, this expertise may be skewed toward specific types of games, leading to potential blind spots. As a result, certain design requirements—particularly those relevant to unfamiliar game types or retelling styles—may be overlooked. This bias is partially mitigated through expert review, which provides an external perspective on the system's outputs.

\subsection*{Expert Review Limitations}
The expert interviews offer deep, experience-driven feedback, but are limited in scale and diversity. Only a small number of Game Masters are consulted, and their perspectives may not fully represent the wider landscape of game retelling practices. Additionally, as the interviews are semi-structured and conversational, variation in questions and depth of discussion may affect the consistency and comparability of the responses.

\subsection*{The Limitations of Tabletop Role-Playing Games}
A broader limitation of this thesis is that Tabletop Role-Playing Games have been chosen as the primary focus. While TTRPGs provide a rich testbed for narrative retelling, they might limit the generalisability of the findings to other types of games, such as video games and board games. TTRPGs have unique characteristics, such as player-driven narratives and improvisational elements, which may not be present in other gaming formats. As a result, the design requirements identified in this thesis may not be directly transferable to other game formats or retelling contexts. Future research would be needed to explore how these findings extend to other domains.

\subsection*{The Limitations of Artificial Intelligence}
As this thesis heavily relies on generative artificial intelligence, the limitations of these technologies also affect the results. The system uses models for transcription, script generation, and image generation, all of which are prone to specific types of error.

The output of these AI systems is inherently non-deterministic, meaning that results can vary significantly between runs even when the input remains the same. This reduces reproducibility and makes it difficult to draw firm conclusions about system performance. Additionally, AI models often introduce biases \cite{luccioni2023stable} or hallucinations \cite{ji2023survey}, producing content that may not reflect the original input accurately. This is especially problematic in a retelling context, where preserving the intent, tone, and factual details of the session is important.

Image generation models, in particular, may struggle with maintaining visual consistency across characters or scenes, leading to confusing or misleading representations. Furthermore, these models may reproduce stereotypical or culturally insensitive imagery due to biases in their training data, posing ethical risks that require careful handling.

Together, these system-level limitations constrain the reliability and fidelity of the retelling output, and should be considered when interpreting the results of the evaluation.

\section{Chapter Conclusion}
This chapter outlined the methodological approach taken to evaluate the TTRPG retelling system. The study employed a combination of case study research, self-observation, and expert interviews, with both qualitative and quantitative data analysis methods. Key ethical considerations were addressed, particularly in relation to the use of AI and the handling of participant data. Finally, the limitations of the methodology were critically examined, acknowledging constraints in generalisability, system performance, and potential bias. Together, these methods provide a well-rounded and reflective foundation for assessing the design requirements of game retelling tools.
